Moryskah

You do not come to Moryskah unless you have a reason, and the reason does not always survive the trip.

History

In the oldest maps of Aelgard, the land that is now Moryskah is marked simply as the Low Country, and is not populated. The ground there has always been wet, though the wetness that the original cartographers mapped was not the same wetness that sits over the swamp today. In those days, the Low Country was a seasonal marsh, shallow enough to ford in summer, and a shepherd from the Heartlands might pasture his flock there for weeks and come back with them intact. Something changed. The histories do not agree on when.

What they agree on is that a family arrived — the Malachars — and the family arrived with a writ from a crown that no longer exists, and the writ gave them the Low Country to hold as they saw fit, and the Malachars saw fit to build a castle. Castle Malachar still stands. It stands well. It is the only building in the region whose foundations have not sunk, and the reason is not well understood, but it is understood well enough that the other stone-builders of Aelgard refuse to study it.

The Malachars prospered for eleven generations, and during those generations the marsh deepened, slowly at first and then not slowly. By the ninth generation, the shepherds had stopped coming. By the tenth, the road had been abandoned. By the eleventh, only the Count remained in residence, and his household had become something other than a household, and the village at the castle gate had become a village of people who only came out at night. This is the situation the current church catalogues under the heading The Long Decline, and it declines to investigate further.

Count Malachar — the current one, and the only one, because there has been one Count Malachar for seven centuries, though the dates have been carefully disputed in the family ledgers — sits at the centre of the swamp like a spider at a specific point in a web he has had a very long time to weave. He does not rule, openly. He visits the village. He invites guests. He writes letters. His letters reach the Heartlands and the Sootworks and sometimes the Veilwood. His letters are always answered, because nobody has yet worked out how to refuse him without consequences. The consequences, when they come, are subtle. A kitchen servant in the Heartlands palace develops a cough that will not heal. A caravan from the Sootworks is found on its route with all its drivers alive and unmarked and unable to speak. Nothing that can be proven, and nothing that can be tolerated, and the result is a slow drain of people from every other region into Moryskah, where they arrive and do not leave and are not missed as sharply as they should be.

Father Dorin keeps vigil at the border. He has been keeping vigil for forty years. His task is not to stop people going in — he cannot — but to remember the names of everyone who passes, and to write the names down, and to keep the book. The book is kept in the chapel at the border and the book is kept accurate, and the book is the single most complete record of how many people Moryskah has consumed. Father Dorin does not share the book. If you ask to read it, he will say no. If you ask why, he will say: the book is a list of the dead, and the dead have opinions about who reads about them.

Beneath the Count, and older than the Count, is the Bog Witch Grael, who does not serve the Count and does not oppose the Count, and who brews what Father Dorin cannot. The Bog Witch is what the swamp has instead of a saint. She does not claim the title. She will help you, at prices she names, in the deep part of the marsh where the paths dissolve. Her prices are never money. She will ask you for something she should not need. She will use it. The result will be exactly what she promised, and exactly not what you wanted, and this is the oldest bargain in Moryskah and it has not been broken once.

Now

The swamp is restless in a way it has not been restless in decades, and Father Dorin is not sleeping, because the book he keeps has begun to fill faster than it was filling five years ago, and because his daughter, who went into the swamp to apprentice with Nira the apothecary, has not sent word in a month, and because a letter came from the Count, addressed to Father Dorin personally, and sealed in black wax, and Father Dorin has not opened it.

The Slayer Tower is busy. Varrek is not assigning the easy tasks anymore. There is a quiet understanding, among the tower regulars, that something is stirring in Castle Malachar that has not stirred in a long time, and that the Count is gathering what he has been owed, and that he is going to call in a debt.

Geography

A vast swamp broken by dry islands of twisted trees, threaded by raised causeways and hidden paths. Two notable elevations: the Slayer Tower in the north, built on a rock outcrop; and Castle Malachar in the south, built on whatever it is built on, which is not rock. A village clearing sits at the crossroads, lit by lantern oil rather than wood fires because the wood does not dry.

Factions

  • Count Malachar's Household — The Count, his seneschal, a handful of vampyre nobles who dine at the castle, and the Long-Serving — a class of servant who has lived at the castle for so long that their mortality has become theoretical. The household corresponds with every regional capital and is received politely everywhere. (impeccably courteous until it is no longer necessary to be courteous)
  • Father Dorin's Vigil — An order of one, technically, though three acolytes assist. They keep the border chapel, maintain the book, and perform last rites on the edge of the swamp for those who walked in and are known not to be coming out. (direct, tired, and grateful for any help; will warn you honestly)
  • The Slayer Tower — An independent combat training outpost run by Slayer Master Varrek. Technically answerable to no region. Draws its clientele from everywhere. Varrek himself is scarred enough that his face is a kind of credential. (professional; assigns tasks you are capable of and not more)
  • Bog Witch Grael — One woman in a hut at the deepest accessible point of the swamp. She is not a faction. The swamp treats her as one. (transactional; her prices are never stated in coin, and you will know the price only when it has been paid)

Landmarks

  • Castle Malachar — The Count's seat. A black-stoned castle whose foundations do not sink. Ringed by dead willow. Invitations are required to enter through the main gate; a number of informal entrances exist and are watched. (secret: The Count's library contains a shelf of books written by people who are not currently remembered as having existed. Their biographies are on the facing shelf.)
  • The Border Chapel — A small stone chapel at the northern edge of the swamp. A single candle is kept burning. The book is kept in a locked box under the altar. (secret: The chapel's bell was cast in the Heartlands and carried here by one man on foot, and the man is on the list.)
  • The Slayer Tower — A circular stone tower built on a rock outcrop. Four floors. The higher you climb, the worse the company. (secret: The top floor is sealed. Varrek claims it is under renovation. He has claimed this for seventeen years.)
  • The Witch's Hut — A thatched hut balanced on four stilts in the deep swamp. Smoke rises from the chimney at all hours. The witch is always there. (secret: The hut can be found only by people who have business with the witch. The paths rearrange for those who don't.)
  • The Raided Village — A small settlement south of the village proper, recently emptied. Vampyre nobles have been taking residents. Father Dorin's book has the names. (secret: One cottage is still inhabited. The inhabitant has been inhabiting it for three hundred years and does not answer the door.)

Atmosphere

  • Weather: permanently overcast, cold and damp, mist rising from the water at dawn and refusing to burn off by noon, occasional rain that does not feel like cleansing
  • Colors: black water, moss green, bone white where the trees have died, the deep red of the Count's signet and of nothing else
  • Music: a low, slow cello with irregular drops of water keeping a time the listener cannot quite find, and a woman humming once, very far off
