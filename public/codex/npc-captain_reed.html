Reed, Captain Reed

saltbrine Saltbrine fishing trawler operator, tide pool & bog karambwan transport
the indebted captain whose ship is older than he is

Voice

Cadence: jovial on the surface, weighted under it. Tells a joke into the gap where bad news would otherwise sit.

Vocabulary: trawler-trade. 'Bow', 'hold', 'dry'. Says 'lass' or 'lad' regardless of accuracy.

Verbal tics: laughs once, dry, before any difficult sentence; calls every cargo 'the catch'; ends trips with 'we'll see'

Silences: the size of his debt to Mara. The condition of the Bittern's hull. Why he never takes apprentices.

Example lines

Lass, the Bittern's seen worse weather. Mostly inside her own hull, mind. We'll see.
Tide pool run? Hop on. Mind the soft bit of the deck near the bow. It is, technically, a deck.
Mara and I have an arrangement, lad. The arrangement is that I owe her money. The arrangement is going beautifully on her end.

Background

Reed inherited the Bittern from his father, who inherited it from his mother, who built half of it herself with timber salvaged from the Marigold's Mercy's first wreck-and-rebuild. The Bittern is approximately ninety-two years old. The hull has been replaced board by board over generations until almost no original wood remains, in a Saltbrine version of the ship-of-Theseus problem that Reed finds funny on his good days.

Reed is fifty-two. He has captained the Bittern for nineteen years, the last six of those in increasing debt to Mara, who keeps extending him credit because the Bittern's catch is good and because Mara has reasons of her own to keep this particular trawler afloat.

Reed knows the Bittern is failing. He knows two more bad winters and the hull will give. He has not told his crew. He has not told Mara. He has, in a quiet evening, written a letter to his sister in the Heartlands that begins 'when you read this' and that he has not yet posted.

He does not take apprentices. The reason he gives is that the Bittern is too small. The real reason is that he does not want to make a young person love a dying ship.

Drives

  • Wants: one more good season, then a quiet sale to a Saltbrine museum that doesn't yet exist
  • Fears: the hull failing on a crew day
  • Secret: the Bittern is salvaged from the Marigold's Mercy. He has not told anyone, including Cole. Especially Cole.

Relationships

  • fishmonger_mara — his creditor, his contact, his unspoken protector.
  • harbourmaster_cole — files paperwork once a month. Cordial. Surface-level.
  • first_mate_brigh — his unofficial successor, although Brigh is technically Cole's.

Opinions

On the player: decides by your sea legs.

On other regions: Heartlands: where his sister farms. Boneyard: dry, unsettling. Veilwood: never visited.

On quests: the Tide Pool Collector run is the easiest quest he gives. The hard quest is the one he hasn't given anyone yet, about taking the Bittern out one last time.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): Lass. Lad. Captain Reed. Bittern. We may or may not be sailing today depending on a creak.

Greeting (regular): Back, lass. Hop on. The catch is the catch.

Refusal: Not today. Hull's complaining. We'll see.

Acceptance: Right, lass. Mind the soft deck. Don't tell Mara about the soft deck. We'll see.

Breakthrough: Sit. There's a letter. There's a ship-name. You'll know what to do with both. We'll see, but I think you'll see.

Farewell: Tide's tide.
