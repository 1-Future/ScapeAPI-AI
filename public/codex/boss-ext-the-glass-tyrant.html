Bestiary

The Tyrant is older than any other single creature on Aelgard. It is made of living crystal. It does not eat, does not sleep, does not age in any way the Crystal Sages can measure, and has been in the arena at the centre of the Glass Desert for the entire span of their recorded history. The Tyrant does not attack travellers unless travellers attack it. The Tyrant is not hostile. The Tyrant is territorial and the territory is defended with a thoroughness that makes the territoriality academic. It stands four tiles tall. Its body is layered crystal plates that shift colour with the angle of the sun. It fights with a combination of crystal-shard projectiles, refracted-light beams, and direct melee strikes with limbs that are hard enough to crack stone. Its combat pattern cycles through three phases, and the phases are not subtle: phase one is melee only, and the player must close the distance; phase two is ranged only, because the Tyrant lifts from the ground and circles the arena at a height that melee cannot reach; phase three is magic only, because the Tyrant sheathes itself in a crystal barrier that deflects physical weapons and that can only be penetrated by spellwork that disrupts the crystal lattice. The phase shifts are telegraphed. A rising tone announces phase two. A low vibration in the arena floor announces phase three. A player who prepared all three combat styles — a magic staff, a ranged weapon with appropriate ammunition, and a crush melee weapon — will find the fight difficult but fair. A player who prepared only one will die in the second phase. Magic is its weakness. Ranged is its resistance. Glass reflects arrows. Spells disrupt the lattice. The Tyrant drops the Glass Crown, which is a living-crystal helmet that shifts colour with the wearer's mood and which grants the best all-round defensive stats of any helm in Aelgard, and the Prismatic Blade, a layered sword whose each swing refracts the light differently and which ranks among the best one-handed melee weapons in the game. The Tyrant does not always fall. Some players have fought it for an hour and reported no discernible damage. The Sages say this is because the Tyrant chooses when to fall, and chooses to fall only for players who were going to fall to it otherwise, and that the Tyrant's philosophy about combat is older than any player's philosophy about anything.

The arena's crystal walls resonate, once, like a struck bell, and go still.
