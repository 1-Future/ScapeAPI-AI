The Glass Desert

The sand here is half-melted. Do not ask what melted it.

History

The Glass Desert is what the east became after the Last Fire. Before the Last Fire, the histories say, it was something else — plains, perhaps, or lightly wooded hills — and the Last Fire burned it to nothing, and the nothing cooled, and the cooled nothing fused, and that is why the sand here crunches rather than shifts, and why the dunes glitter, and why a traveller without a veil will burn her eyes out inside a day. The Last Fire is a vague event in the historical record. Some sources put it at five thousand years ago. Some put it at fifteen thousand. All of them are guessing. None of them are quite willing to say what the Fire was.

Veldrak is what the Fire was. Not the only thing — the Last Fire was a confluence — but Veldrak was the largest part of it. Veldrak is a dragon, the last dragon, and the sleep that holds Veldrak now is the reason the Glass Desert is a place where people can enter at all. The sleep was not cheap. The Crystal Sage Orin will tell you about it in slow, careful sentences, and when he talks about the sleep he uses a possessive pronoun, which is itself a clue about how the sleep is maintained: not collectively, not institutionally, but personally, by a small order of sages who have each held a fragment of the binding in their bodies for as long as they lived, and who trained their successors before they died, and whose chain is now eleven centuries old and is not as unbroken as the histories say.

The Glass Tyrant is older than Veldrak. The Tyrant was here before the Fire. It is a being of living crystal — a creature, not a construct — and its relationship to the dragon is unclear to every living scholar. The Tyrant does not sleep. The Tyrant rules the deep crystal arenas at the desert's centre. It does not attack travellers unless travellers attack it. When travellers attack it, it attacks back. The survival rate is low. The Tyrant has existed, by every available measure, for longer than any other single living thing on Aelgard.

The Fight Caves and the Inferno sit beneath the desert. They are not natural formations. They were built, by whom nobody knows, as a training ground or an arena or a trap, depending on which theory you find most plausible. Ascending the Caves awards the Cape of the Inner Fire, which is the surface world's most durable prestige item. Ascending the Inferno awards the Infernal Cape, which is the same thing but for people who have finished ascending the Caves. Nobody has ascended both without a great deal of help, and nobody has done either without practice, and nobody has practiced without dying repeatedly, and this is considered by the sages to be the system working as intended.

The TzHaar live in the fight caves. The word is their own, not a loan. They are older than the elves and they may be older than the dragons, and their relationship to the crystal is intimate: they are not made of crystal but they are not independent of it either. They forge obsidian into weapons and armour and they sell it, through Merchant Zel at the outpost, at prices calibrated to the wealth of the desperate. Their conversation is limited. Their respect is earned only in their arena, and the respect is worth earning.

This is the endgame region. It is not endgame because the sages made it so. It is endgame because you cannot come here without having survived everything else.

Now

Orin is at the outpost and is older than he was last year, which is the natural direction, but also older than he should be, which is the unnatural one. He has been dreaming the same dream for eleven weeks, and the dream is about Veldrak's eye opening. Zel is busy. The Fight Caves have had twelve successful ascents this month, which is a record. The Inferno has had one. Veldrak is still asleep. The sleep is thinner.

Geography

A wasteland of fused sand and crystal formations, with occasional sharp glass-rock outcroppings. A single outpost at the western edge, sheltered by overhangs. Deeper in, the arenas: the Crystal Caverns (Wyrm country), the Glass Tyrant's Arena, the Fight Caves and Inferno entrances, Veldrak's buried chamber. The sun overhead is white and the sun on the glass is whiter.

Factions

  • The Crystal Sages — An order of eleven, always eleven. Orin is the current senior. Each holds a fragment of Veldrak's binding. When one dies, a successor is chosen. The order does not govern and is not consulted by any crown, but any serious action in the desert routes through them eventually. (patient, specific, willing to answer questions that have a purpose, and silent about questions that do not)
  • The TzHaar — A people of fire-born warriors who live in the caves beneath the desert. They forge obsidian and run the Fight Caves. They do not trade with any surface culture except through Zel. (indifferent until you enter the caves; respectful only if you ascend)
  • Merchant Zel's Exchange — Zel herself, three assistants, and a warehouse at the outpost. Zel trades crystal shards, glass sand, prism lenses, and TzHaar obsidian. She is not a TzHaar but she speaks their trading dialect. (professional; will buy anything from the desert at market rates and sell you anything the TzHaar release to the surface)
  • Orin's Listeners — Not a formal group. A handful of travellers who have stayed longer at the outpost than their business required, and who now sit with Orin in the evenings while he talks. They remember what he tells them. The Sages do not officially sanction them, but they do not stop them. (will nod to you if you sit long enough to be recognised)

Landmarks

  • The Glass Desert Outpost — A cluster of stone buildings under the lee of a large crystal outcrop. A bank. Zel's exchange. A water cistern fed by a spring that should not exist where it exists. (secret: The cistern's spring comes from a depth the Sages have mapped to a door they have never opened.)
  • The Crystal Caverns — A labyrinth of glass-walled tunnels where the Crystal Wyrm lives. Sealed. Accessible only after completing the Glass Prophecy. (secret: The Wyrm is not alone. There is an empty bed in the caverns, nearby. It has been empty for a long time. It is not shaped like the Wyrm's bed.)
  • The Glass Tyrant's Arena — A broad depression in the desert floor where the Tyrant holds court. The ground is smooth. The walls are faceted. The Tyrant does not leave. (secret: The Tyrant's phases — melee, ranged, magic — match the phases of something very old, and the Sages recognise the pattern but have not said aloud what it is.)
  • The Fight Caves — A spiral descent into sixty-three waves of TzHaar combat. Reaching the bottom awards the Cape of the Inner Fire. (secret: The caves have sixty-four waves. The last is not counted because nobody has seen it.)
  • The Inferno — A sealed lower descent. Sixty-nine waves. Ascending awards the Infernal Cape. (secret: The Inferno was not built. It was found.)
  • The Dragon Gate — A doorway at the centre of the desert that opens only when the four regional keys are brought. Beyond it: Veldrak's chamber. (secret: The gate is older than the dragon behind it. This is the Sages' private worry.)

Atmosphere

  • Weather: blinding heat by day, sharp cold at night, occasional crystal-dust storms that can score leather; no rain, ever
  • Colors: white sand, prism colors where the sun catches a facet, a deep rose at dawn, the rare green of living crystal; the ground blazes at noon and burns with the colour of a knife at dusk
  • Music: a high sustained chime over a ground drone that never resolves — beauty that refuses to be consoling
