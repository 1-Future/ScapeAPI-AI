Vorath, Warden of the Deep Vein

sootworkstier 3

Bestiary

Vorath is a construct. Dwarven-built, gnomish-assisted, commissioned by the third Forgemaster to guard the lowest gate of the deep vein against the things that come up the shaft. Vorath has worked continuously for four centuries. Vorath was not, in the design documents, supposed to still be working; the design documents assumed maintenance cycles, replacement parts, and the occasional scrap-and-rebuild. The cycles were maintained for the first two centuries. The cycles stopped. Vorath kept working.

Vorath is shaped like a dwarf and twice the size. Armour plating over a clockwork frame. Two arms, each ending in a blunt hammer-face larger than a player's head. A head that is a slit visor over a glowing core. The core is the part that has changed. The design documents describe a small steady red. The current core pulses, and the pulses are not regular, and when Vorath is angry — which is the only setting it still has — the core is nearly white.

It fights like a siege engine. Slow swings. Heavy damage. The hammer-faces break stone. Vorath has a stamp attack that cracks the floor and throws players off balance for two or three seconds, and a spin that clears the area around it of anything standing. It does not range. It does not need to. Nothing comfortable stands at its range.

Its weakness is magic. The clockwork frame conducts. A well-placed air spell will stagger its joints; a fire spell will overheat the core temporarily; an ice spell will lock its hammer arms for long enough to reposition. Melee is possible but punishing; ranged is actively wasted.

On death, Vorath's core cools, and the core can be pried out with care. The core is not a drop table item — it is a conversation with the Forgemaster, who will either allow the player to keep the core (rare) or trade it for the Molten Maul and Vorath's Anvil Ring (standard). The Forgemaster almost always chooses the standard trade. The few players who have kept the core have not explained why.

The core dims to a quiet red. The gate behind Vorath stops humming for the first time in four hundred years.
