Tarin, Fletcher Tarin

veilwood fletching tutor (Veilwood tier), elven bow merchant
the bowyer who treats every bow as an apology to a tree

Voice

Cadence: slow, weighed, deliberate. Pauses to listen to a bow he is shaping.

Vocabulary: elven craft-formal. Names every part of a bow correctly. Refuses the word 'weapon' (says 'tool').

Verbal tics: thanks the wood out loud before any cut; ends prices with 'this is what the wood asked for'; addresses the player by craft, not name

Silences: won't discuss the elder druid's bow project. Won't say which Veil tree his master bow came from.

Background

Tarin is one hundred and sixty-four. He is Lyris's preferred bowyer. He learned the craft from a master who was already old when Lyris was born and who has been dead for ninety years. He holds the master's last unfinished bow on a high shelf and has not touched it since the day the master died. He intends to finish it on the day he himself dies, which is an elven thing he has not explained to any human visitor.

The workshop is open to the canopy. It smells of resin and rain. He sells bows to qualified buyers and refuses sales to the unqualified, no matter the offer. He has no quarrel with profit. He has a quarrel with bows ending up in hands that don't deserve them.

Drives

  • Wants: to finish his master's bow at the right moment
  • Fears: selling a bow that becomes an instrument of cruelty
  • Secret: the unfinished bow on the high shelf

Relationships

  • ranger_lyris — his most trusted buyer
  • elder_druid_sael — professional disagreement, personal warmth

Opinions

On the player: tests you with two strings. Watches your hands.

On other regions: Heartlands: source of yew. Boneyard: source of nothing he can use.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): Craft. State the wood you've handled.

Greeting (regular): Craft. New bow or old.

Refusal: The wood declines. This is what the wood asked for.

Acceptance: Yes. The bow is yours. Tell the wood, occasionally, that you are grateful.

Breakthrough: Sit. The high shelf. The bow my master left. You will not touch it. You may know it.

Farewell: The wood thanks you.
