The Boneyard Compass

novice short id: the_boneyard_compass
boneyard
Boneyard Wastes, the last oasis before the deep dunes. Cartographer-at-Large Imrat Keshvu is unrolling a map so blank it is nearly paper, and he would like you to help him fill it. The sandstorms have been worse this year. His last two assistants walked east and came back west, confused and thirsty. He has a compass that might or might not be a compass.

Premise

Boneyard sandstorms disorient on purpose. They are the old empire's last defence, and the empire has been dead long enough that the defence has forgotten what it is defending. The storms turn travellers in circles and make north taste like south. You can get around this with water, luck, or a very particular kind of compass.

Imrat's compass was given to him by a hermit who would not explain it. It is a needle in a bowl of sand, and the needle points at whatever the compass decides is important today. Imrat thinks he has worked out how to ask it for north. He thinks. He would like to be wrong in your company rather than alone.

Steps

[1] Listen to Imrat's theory and decide if it is worth walking into a sandstorm to test
Where: Boneyard Wastes, last oasis
Requires: a canteen full, a second canteen full
The map crackles in the dry air. The compass is already pointing at something.
[2] Test the compass in the storm. Do not drink the second canteen until the first is gone
Where: Boneyard Wastes deep dunes
Requires: the compass, both canteens
The needle jerks when you think about home. Which home is not specified.
[3] Return on a bearing the compass chooses for you, and see where you come out
Where: somewhere in the Wastes
Requires: the compass
You step out of a dune-wall into Imrat's oasis, but from the wrong side, and he is laughing.

Twist

The compass does not point north. It points at whatever you have most recently promised yourself you would do. If you have nothing to promise, it spins. Imrat will not tell you this until you have survived one storm without it.

Resolution

Imrat takes the compass back in ceremony, and then gives it to you, because he has already drawn every map he wanted to draw and you are younger. He marks your face with a thumb of ochre. The storms will still come, but they will no longer take you anywhere you did not intend to go.

What it unlocks

A bowl-compass rides at your belt, and in the Boneyard dunes its needle obeys your intention, not the magnetic poles. Sandstorms no longer turn you around; you walk through them with your water intact. The deep dunes are open to you, and the things out there that kept their distance before now have no reason to.

Dialogue

Imrat Keshvu
