The Veilmother

veilwoodtier 4

Bestiary

The Veilmother is the oldest Veil Tree at the centre of the sacred grove. She was sleeping. She is waking. The druids have spent a year trying to put her back to sleep and have failed because they were the ones who woke her, and they did not mean to, and the corruption that began when the Elder Druid was corrupted by something he will not name has spread through her root system in ways that cannot be sutured.

She is not, strictly speaking, a tree. The tree is the form her attention takes when she is not using her attention for anything else. When she engages, the trunk splits, and the roots lift, and she becomes a moving structure four metres tall that fights with her lower roots as lashing whips and with her upper branches as hammers and with her bark, which is harder than stone, as plating. The fight takes place inside the sacred grove and the grove does not make room. Trees get in the way. Stones obstruct lines of sight. The ground is soft where she wants it soft.

She is weak to slash — a blade can cut through wood, if the wood is wood, and she is at least partially wood. She resists ranged, because arrows lodge in her bark and do not penetrate to the living heartwood. Fire works, but fire in the sacred grove is a tactical choice with political consequences that will become apparent the first time an elf sees the scorch marks.

She drops her heartwood, which is a fragment the druids consider sacred and which sells for a great deal anyway, and the Verdant Plate, a piece of armour grown from her living bark that will, with care, mend itself between battles. She also drops the Root Whip, a piece of her lower root system that continues to live after her defeat and that strikes with a reach no ordinary whip can match.

When she falls, the grove falls with her — not all of it, but a ring of her nearest daughters bow their upper branches as if acknowledging something, and they remain bowed until the player has left. The druids do not accompany players out of the grove after the Veilmother's defeat. They do not speak until the player is beyond the forest's edge. What they are doing during that silence is not the player's business.

A single long sigh from everything that had been listening, and then the grove settles into a kind of quiet that had not been there before.
