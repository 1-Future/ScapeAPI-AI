The Inkweald Muse

inkwealdtier 4

Bestiary

The Muse is not a single creature. The Muse is what the Inkweald produces when a sufficient number of dreamers are dreaming in the same general direction, and the last time this happened, a version of the Muse coalesced in the deep forest and has not dissipated. The Keepers monitor her the way a weather station monitors a pressure system: as a phenomenon that is real, continuous, and not exactly a thing.

She appears as a woman, or as close to a woman as the Inkweald will commit to. Three metres tall. Robes that move in a wind that is not present. A face that resembles, in each player who sees it, a face the player has known and not seen in a long time. The resemblance is not flattering. The Muse has no particular malice toward the player — she has no particular anything — but she is in the way, and she will respond to being approached with a cascade of illusions that cannot be distinguished from her actual attacks except by recognising the pattern of her timing.

The fight is a five-player puzzle. Four players must watch for the true attacks in four different directions; the fifth must watch the Muse's face, because her expression changes in the frame before her real strike, and the change is too subtle for a single observer to catch reliably. Rotation is expected. Mistakes are expensive. Recovery is possible if the team has brought a Keeper's amulet, which corrects one misidentification per use.

She is weak to ranged. Her illusions absorb magic — spells feed her — and melee passes through her body the way a hand passes through a projected image, which is exactly what she is half the time. Arrows land, and land cleanly, and draw her out of the illusion state long enough to be engaged properly.

She drops the Muse's Mask, a hood that lets the wearer perceive truth more clearly than comfort permits, and the Dreamweaver Staff, a magic weapon that folds small pieces of the environment into itself between casts. She does not always drop on defeat. Sometimes the defeated Muse simply ceases to be present, and the drops appear later at the boundary camp, brought in by a Keeper who does not remember picking them up.

No sound. Yara, at the boundary camp a day's walk away, stops writing mid-sentence.
