Dorin, Father Dorin

moryskah prayer tutor, exorcism contact, hidden crypt key-holder
border priest who tends a fading flame and a small private heresy

Voice

Cadence: measured. Sentences trimmed to fit the breath of the dying. Pauses are doctrinal — he treats silence as a sacrament.

Vocabulary: scripture-soaked but earthy. Refers to 'the Light' rather than naming the god. Calls evil 'the cold'.

Verbal tics: says 'and so' to begin difficult truths; ends prayers with 'so it stays lit'; touches his collar before saying a name aloud

Silences: won't discuss the schism of the southern altars. Won't explain why the Heartlands Bishop has not visited in nine years.

Example lines

And so we keep the lamp. Not because the dark cares, but because we do.
The boy died with his eyes on the Light. Tell his mother that. Do not tell her the rest.
Lord Malachar was once a postulant of this very chapel. We do not erase the names of those who fail. We turn the page.

Background

Dorin took orders late, after a first life he never quite explains. He came to the Chapel of the Last Light at thirty-two, replacing a priest who had walked into the swamp one autumn morning and not come back. That was forty-one years ago. He has never moved on, refused two promotions, and outlasted three bishops.

The Chapel sits at the literal border between the Heartlands and Moryskah. Half the pews are for the warm and the living; the other half are kept clean for the unquiet. Funerals at the Chapel are not always for bodies. Sometimes the parishioners are smoke, sometimes hunger, sometimes a word that needs putting down. Dorin does the work either way.

Beneath the chapel is the hidden crypt — a memory the Bishop of the Heartlands would rather forget. Dorin knows about it. He has the key. He has not used it in twenty-one years and intends not to use it again, but he renews the wards annually because intention and certainty are different things.

He was once Lord Malachar's instructor in the Light, when Malachar was a thirteen-year-old postulant with a thin face and a serious manner. Dorin remembers him pleasant and quick. He does not pretend Malachar was always evil. He thinks about this on the cold nights and writes nothing down.

Father Dorin keeps a small private cellar of red wine, ostensibly for the sacrament. Hilde knows about it. He knows she knows. They have an understanding.

Drives

  • Wants: to die in the chapel without anyone needing to bury him alone
  • Fears: Malachar coming back through the chapel door, smiling
  • Secret: he has refused, in writing, to consecrate ground in the Castle Malachar foundations — and the Bishop has hidden the letter

Relationships

  • lord_malachar — former pupil. Will not speak the name unless required by the Light.
  • mirelda_bog_witch — they are, technically, theological enemies. Have shared bread three times in fifty years.
  • captain_alden — the captain comes to the chapel on the longest night to burn names. Dorin lets him in alone.
  • merchant_hilde — she resupplies his wine. Neither of them calls it that.

Opinions

On the player: looks at your hands first, then your eyes. Decides whether you have killed something that was already dead, and how recently.

On other regions: Heartlands: the warmth that lets the chapel exist. Veilwood: an older Light, no less true. Inkweald: a place where the Light bends but is not extinguished. Glass Desert: a Light made into a mirror — dangerous.

On quests: the work of the Light is not interrupted by the schedule of heroes.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): Come in. Mind the threshold — it is older than I am and prefers respect.

Greeting (regular): Back, child. The lamp is lit. Sit a while.

Refusal: Not yet. Perhaps not ever. The Light decides, not I.

Acceptance: And so. Take this candle. Do not let it touch the swamp water. Return it to me, lit or unlit, but return it.

Breakthrough: Sit. There is a key. There is a door. You have earned the question of whether to open it.

Farewell: Walk in the warmth.
