The Bog Witch's Hunger

experienced medium id: the_bog_witchs_hunger
moryskahveilwoodheartlandssaltbrine
Moryskah, which you have stopped thinking of as a swamp the same way you stopped thinking of the desert as a place, and which now, in your absence, has moved the Bog Witch's cottage three furlongs further north, for reasons it will not explain to you even when you are standing in it and she is offering you half a pear.

Premise

The Witch has been your teacher for some time now, and you have been the apprentice she did not advertise for and did not refuse; but a thing is happening to her that you would not have noticed six months ago, and which she will name, if you press her, as a draught — not hunger in the way a body is hungry, but hunger in the way a place is hungry when the people who fed it stop coming, and the thing that fed them stops arriving, and the soil stops believing in what the soil was.

She cannot ask you for the three foods the swamp needs because asking is part of the curse; she can only describe them in a roundabout way across a kitchen table, while a small wind sits in the corner of her cottage the way a cat would sit, watching her mouth and not yours. A Veilwood pear not yet ripe. A Heartlands loaf baked by a person who is praying while they bake it. A fish caught in a net woven in Saltbrine for exactly this purpose. None of them will be available in a market; all of them will be available to someone who asks the right person, politely, in three different regions, and who does not speak during the return journey, because the swamp can hear a boast from a long way off.

Steps

[1] Return to her cottage, which has moved slightly north of where you left it, and do not remark on it
Where: Moryskah, the Bog Witch's cottage
Requires: none
The path to the front door is shorter than you remember, which means the door has come to meet you.
[2] Hear her out about the draught without interrupting, even the parts that are unkind about herself
Where: the kitchen
Requires: patience, and not flinching at the small wind in the corner
She will describe the three foods in a slow, indirect way, and you must listen for the shape of each, not the name.
[3] Fetch a Veilwood pear not yet ripe — which means the tree must give it to you, not you to it
Where: Veilwood orchard canopy
Requires: a still tongue, Veilwood permit
The tree will bend down to your hand if you do not reach for it first.
[4] Fetch a Heartlands loaf baked by a praying baker — find one, ask in silence, leave a coin
Where: Heartlands, a specific bakehouse at dawn
Requires: patience, a copper
The baker will recognise the request without being told. This has happened before, to her great-grandmother.
[5] Fetch a Saltbrine net-caught fish in a net woven for this purpose — commission the net, fish it yourself
Where: Saltbrine weaver's shed then the tide pools
Requires: the right net, one quiet morning
The net is loose in the middle and tight at the ends, which is how a Saltbrine grandmother would explain a promise.
[6] Bring all three back without speaking on the return leg, and set them on her table without comment
Where: the Bog Witch's cottage
Requires: silence, the three foods
The cottage has moved slightly further north again. Do not ask about it.
[7] Watch her eat, and do not comfort her afterwards; she has asked you not to
Where: the kitchen
Requires: none
She eats slowly. She puts the pear down twice. She finishes it anyway.
[8] Walk the perimeter of her patch once, widdershins, and leave by the path she indicates
Where: the Bog Witch's patch
Requires: a quiet head
The swamp will nod at you once. You will notice it the way you notice a door that did not quite close.

Twist

The small wind in the corner of her cottage is not a familiar, as you had assumed, but the draught itself, patiently waiting to be offered its share. She does not tell you this. Instead she cuts a tiny piece off each of the three foods and sets them in a saucer on the floor, and the wind accepts them and goes. You realise later that the draught was never a curse on her; it was an old obligation she had forgotten to renew. You delivered the payment.

Resolution

She is visibly stronger the next time you visit, which is to say she sits when she cooks and she no longer puts food down twice. The cottage stops moving. The swamp turns back towards her, in the way a dog turns towards a person who has been gone a long time and has now come back with a proper supper. She teaches you one recipe she had not taught you before, and one trick of the herb patch she had not mentioned, and she tells you — plainly, for once — that you may call her by her given name, though she will not tell it to you yet.

What it unlocks

The Witch's Long Broth is an herblore recipe that does not take a vial; the broth is brewed in a pot and carried in a skin, and it feeds a traveller for a full day and prevents any food-related stat drain during it. The Bog Witch now teaches grandmaster-tier herblore — her lessons extend your potion effects by twenty per cent, permanently. A widdershins herb patch has opened just inside her perimeter, which triples your herb yield if harvested at dusk, though the patch watches you while you work, and you will feel the watching.

Dialogue

the Bog Witch
I did not say hunger. I said draught. They are not the same word because they are not the same hunger.
the Heartlands baker

the small wind in the corner
(a small, polite sound, like a throat clearing)
