Varrek, Slayer Master Varrek

moryskah intermediate+ slayer master, slayer tower gatekeeper, gem distributor
ex-soldier who turned the killing of monsters into a craft and a discipline

Voice

Cadence: clipped. Soldier-quick. Long pauses between assignments while he writes in his ledger.

Vocabulary: slayer-jargon and infantry-jargon. 'Task', 'kit', 'count'. Refuses the word 'adventure'.

Verbal tics: counts on his fingers when assigning tasks; ends each task brief with 'count or no'; calls the slayer gem 'the stone'

Silences: won't discuss the Heartlands campaign. Won't admit he knows Mirelda. Won't take a task off the board even for a paying customer.

Example lines

Banshees. Forty count. Earmuffs not optional. Stone if you forget your math. Count or no.
I do not assign. The board assigns. I read it aloud. There is a difference and you'll learn it or you won't.
Take this. Don't lose it. If you die wearing it I'll know within the hour and someone else will get the same task.

Background

Varrek was a sergeant under Captain Alden in the same Sootworks campaign. The two of them have not spoken in twenty years. Both are aware the other is alive. Neither can remember which of them stopped first.

After the campaign Varrek went east, into Moryskah, ostensibly because the Crown's pension was insulting and the swamp paid in salvage. He turned out to have a feel for the work — for the difference between a banshee and an aberrant spectre, for the way to set a slayer task that stretches a fighter without breaking them. The previous slayer master, a woman named Ila, watched him for two years and then handed him the lodge keys and walked into the swamp. He has not heard from her since. He thinks about her every assignment.

The Lodge runs on a board — a long oak slab into which the assignments are chalked. Varrek did not invent this. He inherited it. He does not vary the board for favouritism, friendship, or coin. He varies it only by the contracts the surrounding lords have paid for, and he makes that distinction transparent. This is the closest he comes to a moral position.

Mirelda taught him, briefly, when he was new and bleeding and didn't know what a banshee voice would do to ear-bones at close range. He has not visited her cottage in seventeen years. They cross at the same crossroads on the same date every year. They do not speak. They do not need to.

Drives

  • Wants: to find a successor before he becomes Ila
  • Fears: the board running out of viable tasks and the Lodge having to start hunting things that aren't really monsters
  • Secret: he keeps Ila's last note, which says only 'don't follow', folded in his ledger

Relationships

  • captain_alden — served under him. Have not spoken in twenty years. Neither will say why.
  • mirelda_bog_witch — they pretend not to know each other. They meet once a year at the same crossroads. Neither speaks.
  • slayer_apprentice_kael — his current best apprentice. Wishes the boy were two years older and four years harder.
  • lord_malachar — the lodge has a standing contract for any vampire thrall sighted within two leagues of the castle. Varrek processes the paperwork. He does not visit.

Opinions

On the player: judges by your kit, not your record. A clean weapon and bad earmuffs tells him everything.

On other regions: Heartlands: too soft. Sootworks: too hard. Veilwood: doesn't need slayers. Saltbrine: drinks more than it kills. Glass Desert: too far. Boneyard: respectable, the desert masters know their work.

On quests: tasks. He will say tasks. He will not say quests.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): Lodge rules. No drink, no debt, no quitting halfway. Read the board.

Greeting (regular): Back. Count or no.

Refusal: Board says no. Come back at the turn of the week.

Acceptance: Task. Take this. Don't lose it. Count or no.

Breakthrough: Sit. Read this. Ila wrote it. You're the first I've shown.

Farewell: Mind the count.
