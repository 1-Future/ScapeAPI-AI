The Inkweald

A door. You recognise it. You do not remember opening it. The door is open.

History

The Inkweald is not a forest the way Veilwood is a forest. The Inkweald is a place that has agreed, provisionally, to be a forest for as long as you are looking at it. The trees are tall and pale and they move when you are not watching, and the ground underfoot is either moss or is waiting patiently to be moss, and the air is warm and close in a way that is pleasant until you notice that there is no weather beyond the canopy because there is no beyond the canopy — the sky is not there, only another layer of trees.

No one built the Inkweald. Several people, over the millennia, have claimed to have found it, and their claims have never been quite consistent with each other: one scholar puts the discovery at three hundred years ago, another at three thousand, a third at last Tuesday. The discrepancy is not error. The Inkweald is not a place where time behaves. The scholars who study it have learned to record their observations immediately, because what they remember about their observations will not match what they wrote down, and the writing is more reliable, though not entirely.

The Lucid Keepers are the order who have built a barrier at the Inkweald's edge and who hold it there by constant attention. Yara is the senior Keeper at the current boundary camp. The camp has been at that spot for a variable amount of time. Yara has been at the camp for longer than the camp has existed. This is not a contradiction inside the Keepers' doctrine, which distinguishes between where a thing is and when a thing is, and which holds that the Inkweald does not fully commit to either.

The creatures of the Inkweald are not creatures the way creatures are elsewhere. A dream wisp is a mote of light that has a personality. A thought stalker is something that hunts by sensing you thinking. A mirror golem is what your reflection would be if your reflection got tired of the arrangement. The danger is real: a dream wisp will ignore you, but a thought stalker will kill you, and an ink horror will reformat the part of the forest you were standing in. The danger is also uneven, because the Inkweald's rules change depending on how long you have been inside it, and what you have been carrying, and whether you dreamed about the forest before you arrived.

The Hollow Choir is somewhere inside. Deep inside. The Hollow Choir sings. The song, when it reaches the boundary camp, arrives as a faint pressure at the back of the head rather than a sound, and it reshapes the part of the forest that hears it, and the Keepers have been reinforcing the barrier for what Yara calls recently, which is a word that means less here than it means elsewhere. The Conductor leads the choir. Nobody has seen the Conductor and come back and remembered seeing the Conductor at the same time.

This is what the Inkweald is. It is not a region. It is a permission the world extends to a certain class of impossibility, and the permission is being tested, and the Keepers are the membrane, and the membrane is thinning. You enter at your own risk, and the risk is not that you will be killed, because death is a clean outcome and the Inkweald prefers unclean ones. You enter, instead, at the risk that you will come out changed in a way that is correct for the Inkweald and wrong for the life you used to live.

Now

Yara is not sleeping. The barrier holds. The barrier is not as thick as it was. A scribe from the boundary camp woke up last week speaking a language that nobody could place, and then, halfway through breakfast, stopped speaking it, and has not since been able to describe it. The scribe is fine. The scribe is writing the usual reports. The Keepers have noted the incident and continue.

Geography

An enclosed forest that behaves like enclosed space even though it visibly stretches to the horizon. A boundary camp at the edge where mortals can still make stable shelter. Deeper in, the geometry fails: paths loop, trees duplicate, clearings appear in places they were not before. The Resonance Chamber — where the Hollow Choir sings — is somewhere at the centre, if there is a centre.

Factions

  • The Lucid Keepers — A small order of scholars and sealers who maintain the barrier between the Inkweald and everywhere else. They take rotating shifts that are measured not in hours but in attention. Yara is the senior Keeper at the present boundary camp. (helpful and specific; they will tell you exactly what is safe and exactly what is not, and they are often right)
  • The Scribes' Grove — A sub-order of Keepers who spend their shifts transcribing everything that happens at the camp, because memory is not reliable here. Their records are kept in a vault that is kept in the Heartlands because the Inkweald occasionally reaches into its own camp and rewrites things. (distracted; always writing; friendly when interrupted, which is often)
  • The Sleepwalkers — Not a faction in any willful sense. People who went too deep and are now walking the Inkweald without waking. There are many. The Keepers try to retrieve them. Few return, and of those who return, few remain retrieved. (passively dangerous; will walk toward you; will swing at you if you get close; show no recognition)
  • The Hollow Choir — The singers at the heart. They are not individuals, or they are many individuals acting as one, or the distinction is a category error. The Conductor leads. (indifferent at distance, directly malevolent at proximity, and neither impression is stable)

Landmarks

  • The Boundary Camp — A ring of lanterns and canvas at the Inkweald's edge. Yara's tent. A small bank vault. A cookfire that does not go out because if it does, the boundary thins. (secret: The cookfire has not gone out in eleven generations. It has been moved exactly twice. Neither move is in the official record.)
  • The Mirror Pond — A pool, ankle-deep, at a clearing about a day's walk in. It reflects clearly. It does not always reflect truly. (secret: The pond answers questions you ask of your reflection, but only once per visitor per lifetime, and only if the question is one the reflection did not expect.)
  • The Scribe Grove — A stand of pale trees whose bark can be peeled off as parchment. The parchment records what is written on it accurately. It also, occasionally, records things that were not written on it. (secret: A parchment peeled from the oldest tree will tell you what you most need to hear. It will not tell you what you most want to hear.)
  • The Resonance Chamber — A chamber at the centre, if there is a centre. The Conductor leads the choir here. The chamber's walls are made of a substance that is neither wood nor stone and that hums when touched. (secret: The chamber's floor has a door in it. The door opens downward. What is below the floor has no description on record.)
  • The Paradox Rabbit Warren — A meadow just past the boundary where small, shifting creatures dart between burrows that are not always in the same place. Their fur grants a single doubled task to anyone who carries it. (secret: The rabbits are the same rabbit. Always. Everywhere.)

Atmosphere

  • Weather: no weather. A perpetual warm dim. Lights seem closer than they are. Sound reaches the camp late, or early, or not
  • Colors: bruise violet, pale ink, the cream of old parchment, a green that is not a plant green, and a single thread of silver visible in everything if you look right
  • Music: a bell ringing underwater, a voice singing the third note of a scale without the other notes, the hush of a library that is empty and was never not empty
