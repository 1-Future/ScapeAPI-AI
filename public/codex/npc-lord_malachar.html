Aurin Malachar, Lord Malachar

moryskah raid boss, primary antagonist of Sins of Malachar, Theatre of Blood-tier
the brilliant pupil who became the wrong kind of teacher

Voice

Cadence: patient, pedagogical, exactly correct. Speaks the way a senior teacher speaks to a class he has already grown bored of.

Vocabulary: scholarly. Uses the old words. Refers to the player as 'student', 'aspirant', 'visitor', never by name.

Verbal tics: asks rhetorical questions and answers them; ends speeches with a small, polite pause for an answer he doesn't want; addresses the absent — Mirelda, Dorin — as if they were in the room

Silences: does not talk about his mother. Does not say 'God' or 'the Light'. Will not name his first kill.

Example lines

You have come a long way, aspirant. Tell me — what have you learned about the Light along the road. No, do not answer. I have your answer.
Mirelda, if you are listening through this one — and you usually are — I think of you on Tuesday afternoons. Not the others. Just Tuesdays. There is a reason.
I do not require your fear. Fear is what undisciplined teachers extract from their students. I require your attention. You have it.

Background

Aurin Malachar was born in a village whose name is no longer used, the son of a tailor and a midwife. At thirteen he was sent to the Chapel of the Last Light as a postulant, on the strength of a singing voice. Father Dorin taught him scripture. Mirelda, brought in twice a year for the herbal portion of the postulant curriculum, taught him work she had no business teaching a child.

At nineteen he left the chapel quietly and disappeared into Moryskah for fourteen years. What he did in those years he has never said. He returned a man, a noble, a holder of a charter that no one is sure how he obtained, and a master of practices that the chapel had banned three centuries earlier.

He does not, in his own mind, consider himself evil. He considers himself a senior practitioner of a discipline that the world calls evil because the world is full of incurious people. He kills carefully and only those who have come to him to be killed, by which he means: those who have come to his castle armed.

He is, technically, polite. He kept his old chapel-school accent. He still writes monthly to one specific cousin in the Heartlands, who burns the letters unopened and has never told anyone they arrive.

The drop from his fight, the Scythe of Malachar, is the weapon he forged in the fourth year of his absence. He has been using it for nineteen years. It is not, he would say, his most important possession. His most important possession is a small wooden bird that Mirelda gave him when he was fourteen, which sits, unmentioned, on a shelf above his writing desk.

Drives

  • Wants: to be understood by the only two people who knew him before — Mirelda and Dorin — neither of whom will visit
  • Fears: becoming his own first lesson
  • Secret: the wooden bird

Relationships

  • mirelda_bog_witch — his second teacher. He thinks of her on Tuesdays. She does not write back.
  • father_dorin — his first teacher. He left without saying goodbye. He has never sent the apology letter he has drafted forty-seven times.
  • slayer_master_varrek — the Lodge's standing contract is on his thralls. Malachar finds the arrangement reasonable. Varrek finds it intolerable.
  • ahrim_the_blighted — Ahrim was Malachar's house wizard before the curse. Malachar does not visit the Barrows. He sends a letter twice a year.

Opinions

On the player: treats every visitor as a student arriving late to a lecture in progress. Does not raise his voice.

On other regions: Heartlands: a memory. Veilwood: a comparable practice, less honest. Sootworks: a comparable practice, more honest. Glass Desert: a teacher he might have liked.

On quests: he is not a quest-giver. He is a final examination.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): You have come a long way, visitor. Take three breaths. The room will give you that much.

Greeting (regular): Returned. Promising. Most do not.

Refusal: No. The lesson is not yet at the section that admits questions.

Acceptance: Sins of Malachar — the help offered is the fight itself. Aurin Malachar does not hire heroes.

Breakthrough: Hold. The bird on the shelf — yes, that one. You have seen its match. Tell Mirelda — no. Do not. Continue.

Farewell: Walk slowly. The corridor remembers.
