Count Malachar

moryskahtier 3

Bestiary

The Count is a vampyre, and the vampyres in Aelgard are not the vampyres of Heartlands folk-stories, which are sad creatures who cannot cross running water. The vampyres of Aelgard are an aristocracy with a biology. They age slowly, feed reluctantly, and reason clearly, and they remember everything. Count Malachar has been remembering things since the Malachar writ was signed, and the writ was signed seven centuries ago, and most of what he remembers concerns the people who were alive then and whose descendants are alive now, and his interest in those descendants is best described as proprietary.

He lives in Castle Malachar, in the south of the Moryskah swamp, in a keep whose foundations do not sink. He receives invitations — he extends them, actually — and he has extended one, recently, to the player, by means of a dead vampyre noble found in the raided village south of the swamp, with the invitation rolled in his hand. The invitation is real. The dinner, should the player accept it, is also real. The Count is a gracious host. He is not a safe one.

In combat, he is patient. He is not slow. He is faster than his size suggests — size two, but with a reach of five tiles — and his attacks drain health from the target and feed it to him, which means that staying at range is not a tactic so much as an obligation. He opens with a mist, which shortens the player's view of the arena for a few seconds. He closes with his hands, not a weapon; his nails are long and black and hardened. He will, at low health, bite, and the bite is harder to survive than anything else he does.

He is vulnerable to silver. Any silver weapon, including the Silver Sickle sold at Apothecary Nira's in the village, does twenty percent more damage. He is also vulnerable to consecrated ground — a vial of soil from the border chapel, prepared in the quest, will briefly immobilise him if thrown at his feet. Slash is his weakness in general terms; ranged bounces off the cloak he wears over his shoulders, which is not a cloak but something flatter and more functional.

He drops the Sanguine Cape (a blood-red cape that pulses slightly), Malachar's Signet (a ring that drains life on strike), and the Bloodwood Staff (carved from a tree that feeds on blood). He does not drop his crown. He will, on his second encounter with a returning player, offer the player a seat at his table, and the offer should be declined.

He says, very quietly, the name of the village his house was founded to protect. The name is not on any modern map.
