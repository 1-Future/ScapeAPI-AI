Roots of the Old Growth

intermediate long id: roots_of_the_old_growth
heartlandsveilwood
Heartlands. The Great Ash at the centre of the old commons is dying in a way the village can feel — the wells are lower, the bees are confused, the dogs will not lie in its shade. Elder Marwen stands at its base with his hand on the bark like he is saying goodbye to a relation. He has sent for the Veilwood druids, but the druids will not come, and he would like you to go and ask why.

Premise

The Great Ash is not ordinary. Elder Marwen's grandmother planted it as a peace sign between the Heartlands and the Veilwood, and its roots cross the boundary in both directions. When it falls — and it is falling — the peace that goes with it is nobody's to renew. Archdruid Elara will not come because the peace was between elders of a previous generation, not her, and stepping into the Heartlands would claim it as hers to keep. She will, however, teach you the Rootsong, which is not a spell so much as an invitation the tree can hear.

The technique requires more than seeds. Five Moonroot bulbs gathered without cutting. A soil restoration potion brewed from those bulbs and sacred water taken from the druid well without bucket. A living branch from the Heart-Oak, cut with the druid's blessing, which is a different cut than a chop. The grafting is done under moonlight, and the field's attention is held by three waves of blighted insects who are not quite the adversary — they are the audience.

Steps

[1] Speak with Elder Marwen at the base of the Great Ash and take samples of its failing bark
Where: Heartlands commons
Requires: a pruning knife
The bark comes off in curls that smell of old paper.
[2] Walk to the Veilwood druid circle without asking the forest where it is
Where: Veilwood druid circle
Requires: the samples
The circle is where you stop walking and realise you have been walking in it for some time.
[3] Harvest five Moonroot bulbs without bruising any, and draw sacred water with no vessel at the druid well
Where: Veilwood forest floor; Veilwood druid well
Requires: farming, careful hands
Sacred water will sit in your cupped palms and not run.
[4] Chop a living branch from the Heart-Oak with Elara's blessing; the cut must be made on the in-breath
Where: Veilwood, Heart-Oak
Requires: woodcutting, Elara present
The branch falls quietly. Elara does not watch it fall.
[5] Return to the Heartlands and perform the grafting ritual under moonlight while three waves of blighted insects test your focus
Where: Heartlands commons
Requires: herblore, combat, a lit brazier
The insects do not come for the tree. They come for the ritual. You are third in their priorities.
[6] Speak with Elder Marwen at dawn. Let the tree speak first if it wants to
Where: Heartlands commons
Requires: silence
The Ash puts out a leaf, then two. Marwen does not say anything for a long time.

Twist

The sacred water will not be yielded by the druid well unless you first tell the well something you have not told anyone. The well will not tell. The well remembers, however, in a way that means druids meeting you later will know you stood there.

Resolution

The Ash returns, slowly. Elara signs a new peace with Marwen by not signing — by simply not calling the tree back into the Veilwood — and the boundary between the two regions relaxes a little. Marwen hands you the dibber that belonged to his grandmother, worn smooth where her fingers sat.

What it unlocks

You carry a druid's planting dibber that places seeds at their proper depth without being asked. The Heartlands herblore-farming flow is smoother; your seeds take at a slightly higher rate, and the Veilwood herb patches are open to you as proper work, not as trespass. The Rootsong's first verse is yours to hum when a field is sick; it will not cure the field, but it will buy you the time to cure it.

Dialogue

Elder Marwen
My grandmother planted it on the day the war ended. My father tended it. I have watered it. I would rather not bury it. Please.
Archdruid Elara
I will not come. To come is to claim. The peace was theirs. It is yours now, and it is hers, if she will take it. Not mine.
