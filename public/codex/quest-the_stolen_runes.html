The Stolen Runes

novice short id: the_stolen_runes
heartlands
Heartlands. The rune shop on Tannery Row was robbed last night, and the robber took nothing valuable — they took the three cheapest stacks on the counter and left the expensive ones untouched. The shopkeeper, Ruven Mourn, is more offended than frightened, and he has the right instincts: if you steal the cheap runes, you are planning something that needs a lot of them and cannot afford to be noticed buying them.

Premise

Ruven has been selling runes in the Heartlands for thirty-one years. He knows every regular by the way their coin lands on the counter. He also knows a bad spell when he hears it being prepared, and somebody is preparing one: the pattern of stolen stacks (earth, air, mind) is a beginner's binding. Somebody inexperienced is trying to tie down something they should not.

The thief dropped a pouch on the way out the back door. A rune pouch, stitched in the old style, with three inner chambers. It belonged to Ruven's own apprentice, long gone. He does not want to say the name. He wants you to find her before she casts whatever she is casting, and bring her home.

Steps

[1] Examine the counter, the back door, and the pattern of missing stacks. Tell Ruven what you think was being planned
Where: Heartlands, Tannery Row rune shop
Requires: none
The till is untouched. The shelf-dust tells you more than the ledger does.
[2] Find the apprentice, using only what the dropped pouch tells you and what Ruven will not say
Where: Heartlands outskirts, a cottage nobody visits
Requires: the dropped pouch
The apprentice's mother still lives. She will not like being visited.
[3] Stop the binding without breaking it. Break a binding badly and whatever is inside comes out angry
Where: an abandoned chalk circle in the woods
Requires: a flask of river water, and patience
The circle is half-drawn. You arrive exactly as she reaches for the final rune.

Twist

The apprentice is not binding a demon. She is trying to bind her own grief, which is a creature of sorts, and it does not want to be bound. You must decide whether to let her finish — unorthodox, ill-advised, but hers to try — or convince her to walk back with you and tell Ruven why she left in the first place.

Resolution

Either choice sends her home. Ruven waits in the shop doorway, arms crossed, and when she reaches him his crossed arms come apart. He hands you a pouch that isn't the dropped one — it is his own, much finer, and he has not stitched one in twenty years. He also tears up the old ledger and writes your name on the first page of a new one, with a discount beside it.

What it unlocks

You carry Ruven's Rune Pouch — three inner chambers in supple leather, stitched with his sigil, and it holds three different runes without their ever mixing. The rune shop knows your face now, and when you push coins across the counter a quiet tenth of them is pushed back. Nobody else in the Heartlands gets that price.

Dialogue

Ruven Mourn
They took earth. Air. Mind. The rest they left. That's not a thief, that's a student, and a sad one.
the apprentice
If I do not bind it I will carry it forever. If I bind it wrong it carries me. There is no good option. Only mine.
