The Green Thumb

novice medium id: the_green_thumb
heartlandsveilwood
Heartlands farmstead. Farmer Aldwin's beet crop is dying a colour beets should not die. The black crust is spreading from row to row. Aldwin has tried lime, ash, and a priest. Nothing. The old herb-woman who visits his mother says the cure is in Veilwood, and the elves will not sell it, and Aldwin is too old to crawl through a forest that does not like visitors.

Premise

Veilwood does not sell soil. Soil is not, to an elf, a thing that can be owned or traded. It is a relationship. You do not cure a sick field by buying a bag of Veilwood earth, because the bag would sulk in your cart and refuse to wake up when you tipped it out. You cure a sick field by bringing the field to the Veilwood, or by bringing a Veilwood druid to the field, or — and this is the real answer — by learning the Rootsong, which is a way of asking a field to remember what health felt like.

Elder Marwen knows that the Rootsong exists. He does not know it himself. The druid Archdruid Elara knows it and will teach you, reluctantly, if you bring her proof that the field wants to be healed. Proof, to a druid, is five Moonroot bulbs pulled up by someone the forest did not attack on the way in.

Steps

[1] Walk Aldwin's field with him at dawn. Take a bark sample from the great ash at the edge of it — the ash is dying too, just slower
Where: Heartlands farm, the Great Ash
Requires: a pruning knife
The bark peels like old paper. Beneath, the wood is streaked black.
[2] Reach the Veilwood druid circle without being turned around by the forest. This means walking softly and talking less
Where: Veilwood druid circle
Requires: your samples, a mouth you can keep shut for an hour
The moss reads the soles of your boots. You are being evaluated every step.
[3] Pull five Moonroot bulbs from the forest floor without bruising the skin of any
Where: Veilwood forest floor, south of the circle
Requires: careful hands
A bulb turns in your fingers and looks at you. It will not do that twice, if you handle it right.
[4] Brew the soil restoration potion under Elara's watch, then chop a living branch from the Heart-Oak with her blessing
Where: druid circle, and the Heart-Oak
Requires: bulbs, sacred water, a bowl
The water is sacred because Elara said so. That is enough.
[5] Perform the grafting ritual at moonlight on Aldwin's field. Do not let the first pass of blighted insects spoil it
Where: Heartlands farm, under a near-full moon
Requires: the branch, the potion, patience
The insects come in three waves. The field is listening. You do not stop singing between waves.

Twist

Elara will not teach you the full Rootsong. She teaches you the first verse. The second verse must be learned from the Heart-Oak directly, which means asking a tree a question and waiting until it answers, which takes longer than you have. The field recovers without the second verse, but you will notice the lack later, in another farmer's field, when you go back.

Resolution

Aldwin's beets come back green, slowly. The Great Ash puts out leaves it had given up on. Elara pins a seed pouch to your belt — a dibber that places seeds at the correct depth without your having to think about it — and tells you the Veilwood herb patches are open to a hand that has sung for them.

What it unlocks

Veilwood is open to you as a place to grow things in, and the herb patches there accept your seeds as properly offered. You carry a druid's planting dibber that does not miss. You also know the soil restoration potion recipe, which keeps a field from getting sick for a full hour of watching — enough time to run a crop through without disease.

Dialogue

Farmer Aldwin
Priest came. Said a blessing. Beets got worse. I do not hold him responsible, but I noticed.
Archdruid Elara
You do not buy a cure. You borrow the memory of health and give it back in spring. If you keep the memory for yourself you will not notice, at first, that your own colour is worse.
