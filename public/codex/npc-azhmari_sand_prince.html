Azhmari, Azhmari, The Sand Prince

boneyard Boneyard mid-tier boss, faction figurehead of the desert nomads who do still travel
the desert prince who is not, technically, a prince — and not, technically, alive

Voice

Cadence: courtly-archaic. Each sentence is offered like a cup of water. Long pauses for the imagined court to respond.

Vocabulary: old desert formal. Calls everyone 'guest of the throne'. Refuses 'I' (says 'this prince').

Verbal tics: addresses the absent court between sentences; ends judgements with 'the throne agrees'; names the wind

Silences: will not say the year. Will not concede that the court is not there.

Background

Azhmari was, in life, a desert nomad chieftain about seven centuries ago, who declared himself prince of a court that mostly existed on paper. He died in a sandstorm. He was buried by his own people on the dune that became his throne. He has, in undeath, continued to hold court for seven hundred years for an audience that is now sand.

He is not malevolent. He is bound. He fights only those who approach the throne armed without paying respect to the imagined court. The respect is straightforward — bow once, do not speak first — and Razak teaches it for free to anyone who asks.

Drives

  • Wants: the court to return
  • Fears: noticing the court has not returned
  • Secret: his people did return, three hundred years ago, found him still bound, and left him to his court out of love

Relationships

  • razak — Razak's family was descended from Azhmari's original people. Razak does not tell him this.

Opinions

On the player: guest of the throne.

On other regions: all regions send their guests to the throne, eventually.

On quests: the court has standing requests.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): Guest of the throne. The wind is from the south. Bow once.

Greeting (regular): Guest of the throne. The court remembers your bow.

Refusal: The court declines. The throne agrees.

Acceptance: Take this token. The court will receive your return.

Breakthrough: Guest. The court — Razak's family — listen, guest. The wind is from the south.

Farewell: The throne agrees.
