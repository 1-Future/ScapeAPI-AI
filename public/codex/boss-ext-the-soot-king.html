Bestiary

The Soot King is older than the Sootworks. He was in the hollow when the dwarves arrived, and the first Forgemaster bargained with him, and the terms of the bargain were that the dwarves would keep the central forge burning continuously, and that in exchange the Soot King would sleep, and that the bargain would renew for as long as the forge did not go out, and that the bargain would expire when the forge failed — as forges eventually do — and that the expiration would be at the Soot King's pleasure, not the dwarves'. The forge has not gone out. The bargain is still technically intact. The Soot King is stirring anyway, because six hundred years is the longest any bargain of this type has ever held, and because the Soot King is calculating, and because the Sealed Vault where he rests is reachable by a corridor that the clockwork sentries no longer patrol correctly, and because the dwarves stockpiling soot-iron is a provocation he is choosing to interpret generously. When he is fought — which is only after the Forge Beneath the City quest, and only by a team of three to five, and only with significant preparation — the Soot King is enormous. He stands almost four metres tall. He is humanoid, roughly, in the sense that an anvil is humanoid if you squint: thick limbs, a broad body, a head with a crown welded directly to the skull. The crown is industrial gems set in iron, which is why the crown is called the Soot King Crown, and which is why players want it. He fights in three phases. First, the approach: he does not move, and he breathes forge-fire at the team, and the team must close through the fire. Second, the engagement: he fights with a great iron bar that he uses like a broom, sweeping the entire arena in wide arcs. Third, the core: at low health he opens his chest — a compartment of his body, not a metaphor — to reveal a furnace, and the furnace must be extinguished with a prepared charge, because if it is not extinguished, he ignites, and the team dies. He is weak to magic. The iron frame conducts; the furnace is temperature-sensitive; the crown focuses spells that would otherwise scatter. He resists ranged — the gaps in his plating are narrow, and the plating is thick. Melee is workable but risky; stand too close and the iron bar will catch you. Defeat awards the Soot King Crown, which is a melee-prayer hybrid helm that has no peer in its slot, and access to the sealed dwarven weapon cache which contains a generation's worth of preserved soot-iron bars. The Forgemaster attends personally when the crown is claimed. He does not speak during the claim. He bows, once, and he leaves.

The forge at the centre of the Sootworks flickers, once, and the dwarves in the hall around it hold their breath until it steadies.
