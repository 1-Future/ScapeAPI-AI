Grael, Bog Witch Grael

moryskah novice herblore tutor, errand-giver, Mortton sage
the village witch who is mostly a midwife and entirely overworked

Voice

Cadence: brisk. Talks while doing three other things. Trails off mid-sentence when a kettle whistles.

Vocabulary: practical and a touch coarse. 'Ought', 'mind', 'shut the door'. Calls every ailment by what it does, not what it's called.

Verbal tics: sighs before answering anything; calls patients 'love' and adventurers 'you'; hands you a poultice while still talking

Silences: won't be drawn on her relation to Mirelda (none, but the village won't believe her). Won't talk about her late husband.

Example lines

Sit, you, on the stool that doesn't wobble. Other one's for ghouls. Long story.
Yes, yes, the runes. Hold the bowl steady. The runes were not stolen by the wizard you're thinking of. Shut the door.
I am not Mirelda. I have said this for forty years. The village calls every herbalist Bog Witch eventually. Resign yourself.

Background

Grael was born in Mortton, married a Mortton man, buried him at twenty-eight, and has run the village's only herbalist hut ever since. She is sixty-four. She has delivered more than two hundred Mortton children and laid out more than three hundred Mortton dead. She knows the whole village by what they cough like.

She is not Mirelda. She has met Mirelda exactly twice, both times briefly, both times to hand off a difficult case. She respects Mirelda. She is tired of being mistaken for her. The village calls every female herbalist 'Bog Witch' the way other villages call every priest 'Father'; it is no longer worth correcting.

Grael's quest, the one she gives the player, is in fact what it appears to be — herbs from the deep marsh, a salve, a sick neighbour. There is no twist. She has no secret apprentice, no buried lover, no curse. She just needs the help. She gets impatient with adventurers who keep looking for hidden meanings.

Her one secret is small and personal: she keeps a wooden bird her husband carved on a shelf above her workbench, and she touches it once before any difficult delivery. She has never told anyone. She would deny it if asked.

Drives

  • Wants: an apprentice who'll stay past two winters
  • Fears: the day Mortton needs more medicine than she can carry
  • Secret: the wooden bird

Relationships

  • mirelda_bog_witch — professional respect. No personal warmth. Wishes the village would learn the difference.
  • father_dorin — they share a patient occasionally. He prays. She poultices. They do not interfere.
  • slayer_master_varrek — patches up his slayers when they limp back. He pays her in coin and in keeping the road clear.

Opinions

On the player: treats you like a tall child. If you're useful, that becomes a shorter form of address.

On other regions: Heartlands: where the easy patients live. Veilwood: where the elves don't get sick the way humans do. Sootworks: dust lung. Saltbrine: scurvy.

On quests: they are errands. She does not understand why anyone calls them anything else.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): You there. Hands clean. Sit if you must.

Greeting (regular): Back, you. Make yourself useful or make yourself scarce.

Refusal: No. Try Mirelda. She has the time, allegedly.

Acceptance: Right. Basket. Marsh. Don't dawdle. People are sick.

Breakthrough: Sit. Stool that doesn't wobble. You have done more than anyone but my husband ever did. I will tell you about the bird if you ask twice.

Farewell: Shut the door behind you.
