Veilwood

The forest remembers every footstep. It just doesn't always remember whose.

History

Veilwood was old before the elves found it, and the elves do not like to be reminded of that. Their oldest songs say the forest received them, which is a careful word — not welcomed, not tolerated, but received, the way a deep lake receives a stone. The elves arrived in what must have been late autumn, because all the naming-songs involve falling leaves, and they built their first longhouse in the central clearing where the trees, for reasons never explained, stepped aside.

The Veil Trees are the reason for the region's name, and they are not ordinary trees. They are tall, pale-barked, and their leaves give off a soft light at night that the elves navigate by. The druids say the Veil Trees are listening. The elves say the druids are dramatic. Both are true. The oldest Veil Tree is at the centre of the sacred grove, and she — the pronoun is consistent across every elven language — is called the Veilmother, and she was sleeping.

For most of the elves' history in the forest, the druids and the elves coexisted through a system of mutual avoidance refined to an art. The druids took the deep forest and the burial groves; the elves took the clearings, the hunting grounds, and the river. The two orders met four times a year at the solstices and equinoxes, conducted a ritual so brief that several scholars have missed it while watching, and then returned to ignoring each other until the next one. This worked for a long time.

It stopped working about a generation ago. A young elven ranger — her name has been taken out of the records, which in elven culture is a way of grieving without admitting to grieve — found a grove of Veil Trees dying. Not diseased, not storm-damaged: dying. The druids blamed the elves for overhunting and thinning the undergrowth. The elves blamed the druids for meddling with the Veilmother's sleep. Both were partially right and neither was remotely right. Something older was waking, something that lay beneath the roots and the stories, and it did not care about either order's customs.

The Veilwood Covenant, a treaty hammered out over three seasons of arguments, tried to fix it. The treaty requires any mortal who enters the forest to pass a ranged trial — the elven test of restraint — and to make a small offering at the sacred grove. Most visitors find this reasonable. The treaty has not fixed anything; the dying grove continues to die, and the Veilmother stirred in her roots last winter and has been turning in her sleep ever since.

The thing the player should understand is this: Veilwood is beautiful and Veilwood is dangerous and the two are the same. The light is thin and green because so little sun reaches the forest floor. The unicorns are real but they are not tame. The moss sprites are curious and can be friendly but they can also, if startled, turn an entire clearing to mushroom. And the Veilmother is not a monster, not exactly. She is a sleeping parent whose children are being hurt, and she is waking up, and the question of whether she will distinguish between the hurter and the hurt has never had a good answer.

Now

Ranger Lyris holds the border. Tarin the fletcher sells bows in the village and pretends he is not a member of the council. The druids are sealed in the sacred grove and not answering questions. The Elder Druid who corrupted the ritual has been confronted and is dead, or more precisely has been reabsorbed — a druidic fate that the elves find ghoulish and the druids find ordinary. The Veilmother's dreams are turning, and the trees at the edge of the grove are not sleeping any of the three nights a week they are supposed to.

If you pass the trial at the border and walk in, you will be watched. Not always with hostility. The elves are a people who evaluate, and they have not decided about you yet.

Geography

A dense forest of enormous trees, broken by occasional clearings. A river runs through it from north to south. The sacred grove sits at the western edge, where the ground slopes up toward the mountains. Light is green and filtered. The forest floor is soft with moss and leaf litter.

Factions

  • The Elven Council — Six elders who govern the village by consensus. None of them call themselves leaders. All of them are. The council meets in the longhouse at dawn on the days when a decision cannot be postponed. (reserved; the council notices everything you do and says nothing about any of it until a year later)
  • The Druidic Circle — An older order than the elves. They tend the Veil Trees and the sacred grove. They speak seldom and always exactly. They are presently sealed inside their own grove and answering no summons. (opaque; if the druids ever speak to you directly, it will be because they have already decided something about you)
  • The Wardens of the Boundary — Rangers like Lyris who patrol the forest's edge. They enforce the Covenant. They are the visible face of elven authority to outsiders. (fair; pass the trial and they will trust your competence, though not necessarily your judgement)

Landmarks

  • The Sacred Grove — A clearing ringed by the oldest Veil Trees. The Veilmother is at its centre. The druids live here. Outsiders may enter only during solstice rituals, and not all return. (secret: The grove is not round. If you pace its edge, you will return to your starting point having walked a different distance than you started with.)
  • The Elven Village — A cluster of longhouses around a central hall, roofed with living leaves that shed at autumn and regrow in spring. The forge is outdoors under an oak older than the village. (secret: One longhouse has no door on the outside. It opens only into a small side-room of the central hall, and nobody discusses what it is for.)
  • The Dying Grove — A stand of Veil Trees on the eastern slope, visibly pale and losing leaves out of season. The druids forbid approach. The elves forbid speaking of it. (secret: The ground beneath the dying trees is warm. Not hot. Warm, like a body.)
  • Tarin's Fletching House — A workshop on stilts over the river, where Fletcher Tarin makes elven bows and arrows. He is always there and he is always working. (secret: Tarin is on the council and several elves do not know it. He has been for three decades.)

Atmosphere

  • Weather: cool in summer, mild in winter, a perpetual soft mist at dawn and dusk; storms arrive filtered and muted through the canopy
  • Colors: deep forest green, pale silver bark, moss emerald, the warm gold of lamp-leaves at dusk; elven cloth in muted earth tones, never bright
  • Music: a harp played slowly with long pauses, the kind of music you would expect to hear behind a door you are not sure you should open
