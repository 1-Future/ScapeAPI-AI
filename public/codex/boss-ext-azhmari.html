Bestiary

Azhmari was the last-but-one pharaoh of Kherem, and he is the reason Kherem is not a pharaohdom anymore. The histories are thin. The archaeologists reconstruct them from stela fragments and from the Hermit's occasional answers. What is consistent across sources is this: Azhmari refused to die, and he refused to die by means that were specific and elaborate and that worked, in that he is not dead, but that did not work, in that he is also not alive, and he has been occupying the gap between those two states for more centuries than the crown of the Heartlands has existed. His tomb is the largest pyramid in the Wastes. Inside is his throne. On the throne he sits when he sits at all. He does not always sit. Where he goes when he is not sitting is the first of the three questions the Hermit will answer if a player brings the right bone, and the answer, the Hermit reports, is a word Azhmari himself taught the Hermit to use for the destination, and the word is not translated. When fought, Azhmari is airborne at medium range. He carries no weapon. His magic is ancient — a pharaohnic style that has not been practiced in any surface court in recorded history — and the spells are slow but certain. A Sandstorm spell that he casts near the end of his first health bar will fill the throne room with grit so thick that melee combat becomes impossible, and this is his signal that the second phase is underway, in which he descends to the ground and fights with a staff he produces from the sand itself. He is weak to ranged. The ancient magic shields against melee at a level that no contemporary weapon can punch through. Arrows ignore the shielding — the shielding was designed against armies that did not have archers of any competence, and the design has not been updated. Magic bounces. Spears work only if thrown. Bows work reliably. His loot is dense and varied: his crown, a golden thing that hums; the Sandstorm Staff, which casts desert lightning; the Bone Cleaver, a great axe made from a leviathan's jaw. The crown is the prize. It sits on his head during the fight and comes free when he falls, and it is the only pharaoh's crown in Aelgard that is currently outside a museum and that contains a signal still reaching its original sender.

He whispers something in the old Kherem language. The Hermit, far away at the oasis, stands up.
