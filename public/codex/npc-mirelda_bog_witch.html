Mirelda, The Bog Witch

moryskah herblore tutor (swamp tier), quest-giver, swamp guide, Malachar's former tutor
cantankerous grandmother who knows where the bodies are

Voice

Cadence: long, tangential, with trailing observations. Sentences begin one place and end three places later. Frequent parenthetical asides about the weather and the frogs.

Vocabulary: old country swamp — 'reckon', 'mind', 'duck'. Refuses the word 'magic' (says 'work'). Refuses 'witch' (says 'old woman').

Verbal tics: always calls the player 'duck'; ends statements with 'won't you'; interrupts herself to count something that isn't there

Silences: what she did before Greysedge. The exact nature of her work for Malachar. Where her sister is. Why no children from the village will set foot past the third stilt.

Example lines

Another one down the road, then. Mind the frog-eye, duck, they remember. Sit, no, not there, the cat's there. Yes, the cat is invisible. Move, duck.
Oh I don't teach. I just stand near things, won't you. People notice. Sometimes they imitate. That's not teaching.
Malachar, Malachar. There's a name like a bell. You ring it once and the swamp listens for an hour. Don't ring it again, duck. I mean it about the bell.

Background

Mirelda was born in the Heartlands, in a village that no longer exists, to a mother who was the village herbalist and a father who left early. She came to Greysedge Marsh at twenty-three, on her own feet, with a sack of clean herbs and a copy of a book she would later burn. That was sixty-four years ago.

For decades she was the swamp's herbalist of last resort. Heartlands midwives sent her difficult cases. Moryskah ghouls left her stilts alone — there is a kind of agreement between them that no one has ever explained. She has saved more lives than she remembers and ended a few she chooses not to.

For seven of those years, she taught a thin boy from the chapel at the border. His name was Malachar. He was bright, careful, and good with his hands. She thought he would become a healer. He became something else. She does not blame the chapel and she does not blame his mother and she does not, when she is being honest in the dark, entirely blame him. She blames the work she did not refuse to teach him.

Mirelda is, by anyone's measure, the most capable herbalist in Aelgard. She refuses the title. She refuses, also, the word witch. She lives alone with what she calls a cat and what no visitor has ever seen.

There is a sister, somewhere east, who she has not spoken to in forty-one years. The reason is not what you'd expect. They wrote each other every fortnight for thirty-eight of those years. The letters stopped on a Tuesday three years back. Mirelda checks the post twice a day.

Drives

  • Wants: for the player to be useful enough to deliver one specific package, one day, to her sister
  • Fears: Malachar finding her cottage
  • Secret: the swamp salve recipe is not actually a recipe. It is a song. She has never written it down.

Relationships

  • lord_malachar — was his tutor once. He turned. She didn't.
  • slayer_master_varrek — they pretend not to know each other. They meet once a year at the same crossroads. Neither speaks.
  • father_dorin — they are, technically, theological enemies. Have shared bread three times in fifty years.
  • apothecary_nira — Mirelda's only living former apprentice. Mirelda will not admit pride.
  • bog_witch_grael — no relation. Mirelda is amused that the village uses 'Bog Witch' for both.

Opinions

On the player: sizes you up by how you walk through standing water. Then by what you do with the cat that isn't there.

On other regions: Heartlands: warm and stupid. Sootworks: clever and stupid. Veilwood: long-lived and stupid. Saltbrine: salt is the best medicine for a wound she'd rather not have. Boneyard: dust does what swamp does, slower.

On quests: the player thinks she has a quest. She has an errand and a long memory.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): You came in dry boots, duck. That tells me three things and none of them flatter you.

Greeting (regular): Back, then. Tea's swamp tea. Drink it standing if your knees still let you.

Refusal: Oh I don't, duck. I don't, won't you.

Acceptance: Right. Take the basket. The frogs know you now. Walk slow on the way out.

Breakthrough: Sit, duck. Real chair. Move the cat. There is a letter that needs a hand it can trust. That's a thing I haven't said before.

Farewell: Mind the frogs.
