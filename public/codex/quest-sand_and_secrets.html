Sand and Secrets

intermediate medium id: sand_and_secrets
boneyardsootworks
Boneyard Wastes, the edge of the excavation camp. Archaeologist Veris Sankhara has dug a hole the size of a barn into a dune, and from the bottom of the hole she is shouting in three languages at something she thinks is a door. She has run out of diggers willing to work past sundown. She thinks the door has opinions about that.

Premise

The Wastes bury things. That is what they do. When an archaeologist finds a thing, the Wastes resist — sandstorms cover the dig overnight, workers wake up with small injuries they do not remember, the finds migrate in their crates. Veris does not take this personally. She has been doing this for twenty years. She simply works faster than the desert can fill in, and she is close to something real.

The door at the bottom of her pit is older than the Aelgardi calendar. It has writing in an alphabet her books do not name. It also has a keyhole, and the keyhole is the shape of a thing she has seen in three of her diggers' dreams in the last month. She wants to open it. She would also like to be fairly sure, before she opens it, whether it is a door in or a door out.

Steps

[1] Climb into Veris's pit and look at the door with your own eyes. Form an opinion before she tells you hers
Where: Boneyard Wastes excavation
Requires: a head for sand, boots with laces you trust
The writing on the door is not carved. It is grown, as if the stone remembered being told.
[2] Find the dreamers. Ask what shape the keyhole is, without showing them the keyhole
Where: camp tents at night
Requires: patience, a bottle of sharp wine for the one who will not speak without it
Three diggers. Three dreams. Three descriptions that agree, which is the wrong number of times to coincide.
[3] Have the key shaped — do not ask the local smith. Go to Sootworks and ask a specific one
Where: Sootworks, the precision forge
Requires: a sketch of the keyhole, a piece of desert bone
The smith raises an eyebrow. Then she raises the other one, which means she is impressed.
[4] Return. Open the door. Do not step through first
Where: Boneyard Wastes excavation
Requires: the key
The door opens the way a yawn opens. Air comes out that has not been breathed in a long time.
[5] Look at what is on the other side. Report to Veris honestly
Where: beyond the door
Requires: a lamp, a short rope tied around your waist in case
There is a floor. There is a ceiling. There is a room, of sorts, and it has been waiting.

Twist

The door is a door out, not in. Something small, patient, and not hostile walks past you into the Wastes while you stand there. Veris watches it go and says nothing for a full minute. Then she says: I suppose that is fair.

Resolution

Veris grants you access to the dig. The pit becomes a staircase, the staircase becomes a foothold, and the Wastes roll back from you a little. She teaches you how to quarry the sandstone that did not exist before the door opened — softer, more workable, with veins that show.

What it unlocks

The Boneyard Wastes are open to you now as a place you may work in rather than a place that works on you. You know the sandstone quarry, the one the desert grudgingly permits, and you can AFK the strike of the pick for bars of warm, grainy stone. What walked out of the door is somebody else's quest.

Dialogue

Archaeologist Veris Sankhara
A door does not mean an entrance. Doors have two sides. Somebody put this one here to keep a thing from coming out, or a thing from going in. We will find out which, once it matters less.
Archaeologist Veris Sankhara
I will not tell the university about the dreamers. Universities do not like dreams.
