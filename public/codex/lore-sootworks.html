The Sootworks

Breathe shallow. Stand on the grate. The forge runs night-and-day.

History

The dwarves came down. That is the phrase they use. Not up, not in — down. They came down from a mountain that is no longer on any map, and they built the Sootworks in the hollow they found when they got to the bottom of the mountain, and they have been building it ever since. The Sootworks is not a city. The Sootworks is a machine that happens to house a city inside itself.

The first forge was lit in the founder's hall and has not gone out in six hundred years. Forgemasters change. The forge does not. Soot-iron — the dwarves' house metal, harder than steel and darker, with a faint grain like wood — can only be smelted at that forge's heat, and the heat is only maintained by feeding the forge the coal of the deep vein, and the deep vein is a seam that runs so far down that the dwarves stopped mapping it three generations ago because the map was useless. You cannot map something that has no bottom. You can only dig.

The gnomes came later. The gnomes are not native. They arrived from the surface during the Spellfire War, refugees from a war the dwarves were not fighting, and they paid their rent in gears. Gnomish clockwork and dwarven metallurgy turned out to be a compatible marriage. Within a century the Sootworks had clockwork sentries patrolling the tunnels, steam trams running between the main chambers, and a mechanical tally-system that tracks ore production to four decimals. The dwarves do not entirely approve of the gnomes. The gnomes do not entirely approve of the dwarves. The Sootworks runs.

The deep vein is the problem. The deep vein has always been the problem. You can ask any dwarf older than sixty and they will tell you that the vein was safer in their grandfather's day, which is what every dwarf has said in every generation back to the founding, which means either the vein is getting steadily worse or the dwarves remember their childhoods too fondly, and it is impossible to tell which. What is not in dispute is that the rock golems have been more active, the lava beasts have been spawning deeper up the shafts, and Vorath — the Warden of the Deep Vein, a construct the dwarves built and then lost the ability to control — has begun refusing to open gates that it used to open daily.

The Soot King is the secret. The dwarves do not speak his name in front of gnomes and the gnomes do not ask. Sealed in a vault below the deepest working shaft, he is older than the Sootworks, and the dwarves found him when they arrived, and their first Forgemaster made a compact with him that bought the dwarves six hundred years of unchallenged tenure. The compact is expiring. Nobody knows the exact date. The dwarves are quietly overproducing soot-iron, banking it against the time when the compact breaks and they have to fight their way out of their own city.

The player arrives in a place that smells, permanently, of hot iron and coal smoke. The air is warm. The grates glow. The dwarves are polite in a curt way that is easy to mistake for hostility. The gnomes are friendly in a frenetic way that is easy to mistake for instability. Neither is wrong. Both are working.

Now

Forgemaster Brun is working. Smith Hald is working. Engineer Fizz is working on three projects at once and not sleeping. The clockwork sentries on the lower levels have started firing at dwarves and not only at intruders, which is a development the council has not yet publicly acknowledged. Vorath has sealed the lowest working gate. The Soot King is stirring. The compact is expiring, and the dwarves have spent six hundred years preparing for this moment, and now that it is here, they are discovering that preparation and readiness are not the same.

Geography

A vast underground complex of tunnels, forge halls, mine shafts, and steam-tram lines. The main concourse is broad enough for a cart. The deep vein descends indefinitely. Ventilation shafts reach the surface at intervals and are always warm. The Sootworks sits roughly east of the Heartlands, accessible through a wide paved tunnel at the Forge Hall entrance.

Factions

  • The Forgemaster's Council — Seven senior dwarves, led by Forgemaster Brun. They vote. They argue. They make binding decisions by two-thirds majority and stop speaking to each other for months afterward. (transactional; complete a contract and they will pay; fail one and they will not speak to you again)
  • The Gnomish Engineering Corps — Not a formal guild. A cluster of gnome households who trade in clockwork parts and innovation. They keep the trams running. They are terrified of the deep vein and will not go below Level Four for any fee. (enthusiastic; hand them a broken mechanism and they will repair it for the pleasure of the puzzle)
  • The Deep Vein Miners — The dwarves who still work the lowest levels. Older. Quieter. Paid double. They do not eat meals with the other dwarves and they do not sleep in the main dormitories. (distant; they answer direct questions with direct answers and volunteer nothing)
  • The Bound Scholars — A small order of dwarves whose sole task is to read and re-read the Founder's Compact. They keep the original in a sealed case. There are three of them. They are always three of them. When one dies, a new one is appointed within a day. (will speak to you only after you have been to the deep vein and come back)

Landmarks

  • The Founder's Hall — The central chamber. The first forge burns at the back. The Forgemaster's throne — a low stone bench, nothing ornate — faces the forge. Business is conducted here. (secret: The stones of the hall are not quarried. They were already here.)
  • The Steam Tram Concourse — The main transit hub. Trams run on regular schedules to the forge halls, the mine shafts, and the surface entrance. The schedule is kept by a gnome who has held the job for forty years. (secret: There is a tram line on the board that has no listed stops. It runs once a month. Only three people are permitted to board it.)
  • The Deep Vein — The lowest working shaft. Mithril and adamant in the walls. Rock golems in the lift cages. Vorath at the bottom gate. (secret: Vorath is not alone down there. When the gate opens, the things behind Vorath are always exactly the same in number.)
  • The Sealed Vault — Beneath the deep vein. A door of solid soot-iron, taller than any dwarf. Engraved with the full text of the Founder's Compact. The Soot King sleeps behind it. (secret: The door is hinged on the inside.)
  • The Lantern Archive — A library of dwarven metallurgical records, kept cold by a ventilation shaft that reaches the glacier above the Sootworks. Most outsiders are not admitted. (secret: The archive has a section on gnomish history. Neither dwarves nor gnomes know this.)

Atmosphere

  • Weather: constant warmth, dim orange light from the grates, a permanent haze of coal smoke that the ventilation fails to clear
  • Colors: black iron, coal grey, forge-red glow, brass accents; the gnomes favor green enamel; the dwarves favor nothing but the colors of their metal
  • Music: a hammer on an anvil keeping time for a slow wheezing bellows — industry as a lullaby, not a threat
