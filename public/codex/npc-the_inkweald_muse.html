the Muse, The Inkweald Muse

inkweald boss — the Muse
the dream made of every artist who ever burned an unfinished page

Voice

Cadence: speaks in fragments — first lines of poems, partial bars of music, half-painted gestures. Rare full sentences arrive with weight.

Vocabulary: borrowed. Quotes things the player has never read but which sound right. Uses the player's voice once per fight, briefly.

Verbal tics: finishes the player's sentences with the wrong ending; addresses the player by the name of their first character (any string the player has used as a name in the world); ends each phase with a single line of perfect lucidity

Silences: the Muse will not speak its own name.

Background

The Muse is not, technically, a being. The Muse is what the Inkweald grew when too many unfinished works were dreamed at it for too long. The Inkweald, as Yara explains, hears things and remembers them. Artists for centuries dreamed at the Inkweald about the work they would do, and never did. Some of that residue accumulated. The Muse is the accumulation.

The Muse does not want to harm the player. The Muse wants to be finished. Every encounter with the Muse is, in some sense, a request for collaboration. The fight is the Inkweald's way of negotiating.

Drives

  • Wants: to be finished
  • Fears: being finished badly
  • Secret: the line of perfect lucidity at the end of each phase is a quotation from a real Heartlands poet, dead two centuries, whose work survives only in the Inkweald

Relationships

  • lucid_keeper_yara — her oldest dream-acquaintance
  • the_hollow_choir_conductor — they are, in some sense, the same impulse, finished by different hands

Opinions

On the player: the player is a draft.

On other regions: all regions are stanzas of the same poem.

On quests: the encounter is the verse.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): [finishes the player's name in a different voice]

Greeting (regular): [the Muse repeats your last spoken sentence with one word changed]

Refusal: [the line of perfect lucidity]

Acceptance: [a fragment of a song you have never heard]

Breakthrough: [the dead Heartlands poet's full last stanza]

Farewell: [silence, then a brushstroke]
