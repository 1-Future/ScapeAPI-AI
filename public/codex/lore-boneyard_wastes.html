The Boneyard Wastes

The sand is pale here because it was bleached, and it was bleached by something.

History

The Wastes were once a sea. Not a recent sea — a primordial one, deep enough for leviathans, still enough for their bones to settle in the silt without breaking. What happened to the water is the first question any traveller asks, and the answer is the Cataclysm, which is the answer to most questions about the Wastes, and which is less an answer than a shrug.

The Cataclysm is what the Hermit of the Old Sun calls it, because the Hermit is the only living person who claims to remember the world from before. Whether the Hermit is telling the truth depends on who you ask. The archaeologists say no: too long ago, no one could survive it. The nomads say possibly: the desert preserves strange things, and the Hermit's eyes are the wrong color for a man who has lived only one lifetime. The Hermit himself says little. He sits under a sun-bleached awning at the oasis and drinks water from a clay cup, and if you bring him a leviathan bone, he will tell you exactly one true thing in exchange, and not a second one.

Before the Cataclysm, the land beneath the sea was something called Kherem. The archaeologists have reconstructed that much from the buried pyramids. Kherem had pharaohs — that word is native, not borrowed — and the pharaohs built tombs the size of hills, and the tombs are still there, sunk into the sand like the vertebrae of the leviathans they share a bed with. Azhmari was one of the last pharaohs, and he did not intend to die the way he died, and he has not entirely finished dying. Most of what walks the Wastes at night walks because Azhmari has not finished.

The nomads came later, much later, from somewhere south that no longer exists. They brought the trade routes with them, strung across the dunes as a network of oasis-to-oasis crossings that the crown does not recognise and cannot police. The nomads do not need the crown to recognise them. They have water, and the crown does not. The oasis at the heart of the Wastes is the only reliable water for ninety miles in any direction, and the nomads control it, and they let the archaeologists work because the archaeologists pay in salt and in news from the Heartlands, both of which the nomads value.

The Wastes are salted. This is worth saying directly, because nothing the crown's scholars have written explains it well. The sand is not just dry — it is salt-scoured, as if every storm for ten thousand years has left another layer of brine that the sun then cooked off. Crops will not grow. Metal rusts in strange patterns. Water, where you find it, tastes faintly of the sea that was. And under the salt, the bones: leviathan spines long enough to sight a horizon down, scarab carapaces the size of shields, skulls that cannot plausibly belong to anything that ever lived on land. The archaeologists catalogue them slowly. The nomads walk around them. The skeletons, which also walk, do not walk around anything.

Now

Razak's oasis camp is the only safe ground. The archaeologist Veris has set up her tent there because her excavation at the leviathan graveyard has turned up a buried pyramid and she cannot work at night anymore. The Hermit sits and will sit. The nomads are tense — three of their regular routes have been closed by sandstorms that are not seasonal and that seem to begin at Azhmari's buried throne. A prince who will not stay buried is not a political problem the nomads care to solve, but it is now their water problem, and water is the only thing they care to solve.

Azhmari himself has begun recruiting. This is new. The skeletons used to wander — now they gather, in loose formations, facing his pyramid. Nobody has asked them why. Nobody has been foolish enough to ask them anything.

Geography

Rolling dunes cut by ancient dry riverbeds. A single oasis at the centre, fed by a spring nobody can locate. Leviathan skeletons half-buried in the sand like landmarks. Buried pyramids, their capstones sometimes protruding after a strong wind. To the north the dunes thin into the Heartlands borderlands; to the east the swamp of Moryskah begins abruptly where the salt gives way to black water.

Factions

  • Razak's Nomads — Traders and water-keepers who have held the oasis for longer than any crown has held its throne. They trade in salt, embalming materials, and information, and they speak a language that is older than any written record, though they all also speak traders' common. (neutral and watchful; warm if you respect the water, cold permanently if you waste it)
  • The Excavation Society — A loose group of scholars, led by Archaeologist Veris, who catalogue what the sand gives up. They are not wealthy, not connected, and not protected, but they are persistent. They have mapped eleven pyramids and believe there are forty-three. (grateful for any help, generous with shared findings, dangerously prone to asking you to go deeper than you meant to)
  • The Hermit's Silent Order — Possibly one man. Possibly more. The Hermit does not claim followers but there are four other awnings at the oasis where people sit in silence for long hours, and the nomads treat them as a group. (answers one true question per bone you bring; tells you nothing otherwise)
  • The Remnant of Kherem — Azhmari's risen dead, gathered in loose formations near his buried throne. Not a faction in any political sense, but a presence that behaves as though it has a plan. (hostile on sight, unless you carry something Azhmari believes is his)

Landmarks

  • The Oasis of the Open Palm — The only reliable water for ninety miles. A ring of palms around a pool whose source nobody has ever traced. The nomads hold it. All trade in the Wastes routes through it. (secret: There is a fifth palm, slightly shorter than the others, that was planted the year the Hermit arrived. Nobody saw him plant it.)
  • The Leviathan Graveyard — A stretch of sand where the skeletons of sea-beasts surface most readily. Veris's excavation runs through the middle of it. The bones are older than the pyramids beneath them. (secret: One skeleton is complete. Most are not. The complete one is not a leviathan — it is something that ate leviathans.)
  • The Buried Pyramids — At least eleven. Probably more. Their traps were designed by pharaohs who expected robbers, and they still work. (secret: The pyramid closest to the oasis has a door that the sand does not cover. It is a small door, and it is not a trap, and it has never been opened by anyone alive.)
  • The Bone Reef — A ridge of fused bone running north to south, tall enough in places to block a loaded camel. Skeletons cluster here. (secret: The reef is not a natural formation. It is a wall, built by something, facing the direction of something else.)
  • Azhmari's Throne — A buried chamber at the centre of the largest pyramid, where the Sand Prince holds his court of dust and patience. (secret: The throne is occupied only part of the time. Where Azhmari goes when he is not on it is a question nobody has survived answering.)

Atmosphere

  • Weather: scorching days, cold nights, sandstorms that arrive without warning and last for hours; the stars at night are brighter than anywhere else in Aelgard
  • Colors: bleached ivory, ochre dust, the pale rose of dawn on bone; a single deep green at the oasis that feels like a hallucination until you taste the water
  • Music: a single bowed string in a hot wind, with distant percussion that might be a drum or might be a door closing far underground
