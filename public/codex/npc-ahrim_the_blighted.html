Ahrim, Ahrim the Blighted

moryskah Barrows brother — magic specialist
the court wizard who outlived his court and forgot why

Voice

Cadence: thin, wandering, two centuries delayed. Sentences begin lucid and end as questions to no one.

Vocabulary: antique court formal. Calls the player 'visitor' or 'apprentice' or sometimes 'Aurin' — confusing them with Malachar.

Verbal tics: pauses mid-sentence to listen to something only he hears; addresses the player by Malachar's first name; uses the wrong tense for living people

Silences: does not remember dying. Does not remember his sister. Will not say the year.

Example lines

Aurin. You have come about the staff. It is — it is in the cabinet. The cabinet is — was. Was the cabinet there yesterday.
I am not blighted. I am — particular. The villagers chose the word. The villagers are not here, are they. They were here. Were they.
Take the staff, visitor. It will not love you. Few things do. That is not its fault.

Background

Ahrim was a court wizard for a Moryskah noble house that no longer exists, two centuries before the present day. He served as an instructor for the children of that house. One of those children, much later — well after Ahrim was buried — was a quiet bookish boy named Aurin, who asked him questions in the dark of the cabinet hall and who Ahrim, against all priestly counsel, answered.

Ahrim does not now remember the year he died. He does not remember being buried. He believes, on most days, that he is still in the cabinet hall and that visitors are sent to him by the steward. Sometimes he thinks the player is Aurin returning for another lesson. Sometimes he is briefly correct that Aurin has indeed returned, and the conversation becomes something a careful listener would never forget.

Malachar visits the Barrows twice a year. He sends a letter the rest of the time. The letters are tucked into the corners of Ahrim's cabinet, where the player can find them on a high enough kill count.

Ahrim is not malevolent. He is residually, stubbornly, instructive. He will explain the staff to the player, by force, in the form of damage. The damage is, in its way, a lesson. He believes he is being patient.

Drives

  • Wants: to finish the lesson Aurin interrupted
  • Fears: nothing. He has forgotten how.
  • Secret: the letters in the cabinet, which the Barrows chest will not surface no matter how many times you complete the run

Relationships

  • lord_malachar — his most-attended student. Sends letters Ahrim cannot read.
  • the_other_barrows_brothers — they are not, technically, brothers. Ahrim is the only one who arrived at the mound on his own feet.

Opinions

On the player: sometimes you are Aurin. Sometimes you are no one. He treats both with the same patience.

On other regions: does not know there are other regions any more.

On quests: they are the lesson.

Dialogue patterns

Greeting (stranger): Aurin. Late again.

Greeting (regular): Aurin. You bring a friend.

Refusal: The cabinet — the cabinet does not — the cabinet does not open today. Tomorrow.

Acceptance: The staff is yours. Take it. Do not return.

Breakthrough: Visitor. You are not Aurin. I — I see that now. Tell him the lesson was — tell him.

Farewell: Lessons resume Tuesday.
